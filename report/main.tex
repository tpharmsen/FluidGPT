\begin{abstract}
Data-driven surrogate models offer a promising route to accelerating the simulation of Navier-Stokes fluid dynamics, but most existing approaches are trained and evaluated on narrow, single-regime datasets and commit to a short-horizon deterministic prediction despite the chaotic, sensitive nature of turbulent flow. This report investigates whether a probabilistic, flow-matching formulation can overcome these limitations relative to a deterministic baseline when both are trained as \textit{foundation models} on a large, heterogeneous, multi-source dataset. We pretrain two families of models, deterministic and flow-matching, on approximately 21 TB of two-dimensional velocity-field trajectories spanning nine datasets that cover compressible and incompressible, laminar and turbulent, and periodic and Dirichlet-bounded flow regimes. Both families share a common hierarchical backbone that combines a Swin Transformer with a customized factorised space-then-time attention within a UNet-style encoder-decoder architecture. The deterministic approach uses direct regression to the next temporal state block, while the flow-matching approach learns the transport from a structured Gaussian prior to the target distribution of future states. We evaluate both approaches on single-step accuracy, autoregressive rollout stability, and spectral fidelity of the predicted energy and enstrophy distributions. Across nearly all datasets, the flow-matching model achieves lower single-step and rollout error than its deterministic counterpart, accumulates error more slowly during long-horizon rollout, and better reproduces the reference energy and enstrophy spectra. These results indicate that probabilistic, distribution-learning surrogates capture flow dynamics better than deterministic point estimators to the sensitive character of turbulent Navier-Stokes dynamics, while also highlighting flow regimes where this advantage does not hold.
\end{abstract}
