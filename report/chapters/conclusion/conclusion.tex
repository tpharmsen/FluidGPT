\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

This work aimed to build a collection of foundation models for Navier-Stokes dynamics that is both rollout-stable and physically consistent across a wide domain space of flow regimes, relying solely on velocity-field inputs. Secondly it investigated whether a probabilistic, flow-matching-based formulation performs better than a deterministic autoregressive baseline in achieving this goal. The results presented in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} allow each of the three research questions posed in the introduction to be answered directly.

\section{Answering the Research Questions}

\paragraph{RQ1: Single-step prediction accuracy.} The first research question asked whether probabilistic flow matching models can outperform deterministic baselines in predicting subsequent system states when trained on a heterogeneous, multi-source dataset spanning a wide variety of physical parameters and flow regimes. The single-step results in Figure~\ref{fig:ssbox} confirm that this is the case for the majority of the evaluated datasets. The flow matching model \texttt{FM-d2} achieves a lower relative absolute error than its deterministic counterpart on nearly all benchmarks, consistent with the hypothesis that a distributional formulation is better suited to capturing the sensitive solution space. This advantage was not universal, however. On and \texttt{pdegym-sines} datasets the deterministic model matched or exceeded the flow matching model, a discrepancy attributed to a mismatch between the dominant initialisation frequencies present in the data and the frequency initialisation of the flow matching prior. Also, for both models the relative error on the dataset \texttt{pdebench-incomp} turned out to be high, indicating the forcing behaviour was not effectively captured by both model types. All together the benefit of probabilistic modelling is significant but conditional on reasonable alignment between the learned prior and the target data distribution and overall information input.

\paragraph{RQ2: Long-horizon rollout stability.} The second research question concerned how autoregressive rollout error accumulates over time for the two model families, and whether the probabilistic formulation confers greater long-horizon stability across datasets of varying temporal length. The rollout results in Figure~\ref{fig:rollout} show that the flow matching models accumulate error more slowly than the deterministic models on the majority of datasets, producing rollouts that remain closer to a physically plausible regime for longer. Although two datasets tell an opposite story. First, the \texttt{pdegym-sines} dataset again favours the deterministic model, mirroring the single-step exception and reinforcing the frequency-mismatch explanation. Second, external, unobserved forcing in the \texttt{pdebench-incomp} dataset degrades rollout accuracy for all models regardless of formulation, showing that probabilistic modelling improves but does not eliminate the difficulty of predicting dynamics driven by unobserved inputs. Taken together, the probabilistic formulation does confer greater long-horizon stability, most clearly on datasets where the flow matching prior is well matched to the data, and less clearly where that alignment breaks down or where unmodelled physical forcing dominates the error.

\paragraph{RQ3: Physical and spectral consistency.} The third research question asked whether the predicted system states satisfy physical characteristics as measured by spectral analysis, and whether probabilistic models produce spectrally more consistent predictions than deterministic ones. The spectral analysis in Section~\ref{se:spectraresults} directly supports this claim. Across the large-scale, inertial, and dissipation ranges, both models capture the underlying physics with only small deviations from the reference spectra, but the flow matching model's median and IQR sit consistently and significantly below the deterministic baseline's error, both on short spatial scales and over long temporal horizons. This is not only limited to the energy spectra, but also the enstrophy spectra computed on the majority of datasets shows the same pattern, indicating that the probabilistic model also reproduces the rotational, vortical structure of the flow more accuractely than the deterministic model counterpart. As with the previous two research questions, the \texttt{pdegym-sines} dataset is the recurring exception, which can now be explained due to a significant descrepancy in a specific range of wavenumbers on the respective spectra. In conclusion, the flow matching model does not only perform better at a pointwise estimate to the ground truth, it also reproduces spectral characteristics better, which is a more meaningful metric for simulation of turbulent Navier-Stokes systems.

\section{Summary}

Probabilistic flow matching provides a significant advantage over deterministic autoregressive prediction in single-step accuracy, rollout stability, and spectral fidelity. The reason behind this advantage can be attributed to learning a distribution over plausible future states, rather than a single point estimate, which better matches the sensitive character of turbulent Navier-Stokes dynamics. This benefit compounds over autoregressive rollout steps and is visible not only in bulk error statistics but in the detailed distribution of energy across spatial scales. At the same time, the recurrence of the \texttt{pdegym-sines} exception shows us that the advantage of probabilistic modelling is not unconditional, but depends on a reasonable correspondence between the frequency content of the data and the initialisation of the flow matching prior. When this correspondence breaks down, the deterministic model's simpler, more direct mapping can become competitive or superior. 

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Prior specification.} The flow matching model's sensitivity to the frequency content of the target data, most visible in the \texttt{pdegym-sines} exception, points to a limitation in how the prior distribution is currently specified. The prior is not adapted per dataset, and its mismatch with high-frequency, sharply-varying fields such as sine-wave initial conditions appears to directly limit performance in exactly the regime where sharp interfaces need to be preserved. A more flexible prior specification through frequency dependence on the input data could potentially yield better results.

\paragraph{Unobserved forcing.} None of the models evaluated in this work, deterministic or probabilistic, are provided with information about external body forcing where it is present, as demonstrated by the elevated rollout error on the \texttt{pdebench-incomp} dataset. This is a limitation of the input representation used across the whole study rather than of either model family specifically, and it caps the achievable rollout accuracy on any dataset where such unobserved drivers are active.

\paragraph{Comparability of models.} To achieve fair comparability between the two modelling approaches, the model architecture was mostly kept the same, altought each approach may require a different set of conditions to reach its best performance, making a fully fair comparison without quality loss difficult. The flow matching formulation introduces additional components, such as the prior distribution, noise schedule, and number of integration steps, that expand its effective capacity and tuning space relative to the deterministic model. At the same time, the deterministic model may depend on its own set of design choices, for example in architecture or training schedule. As a result, part of the observed performance gap may reflect differences in how thoroughly each model family was optimized for its own paradigm, rather than an inherent advantage of one modelling approach over the other. As the custom model architectures facilitate high variability in model hyperparameters, more engineering work needs to be done to optimize their respective approaches.

\section{Future Work}

Following from the limitations of this work, some continuation ideas of this project are suggested. First, adapting or learning the flow matching prior per dataset, or conditioning its frequency content on statistics of the input data, could close the gap observed on the \texttt{pdegym-sines} dataset and test directly whether the exception is indeed attributable to prior misspecification rather than some other property of that benchmark. Second, incorporating known or inferred forcing terms, for example in symbolic form, as an additional model input would allow a cleaner prediction of system states. Third, extending the depth-scaling analysis beyond the \texttt{d2}/\texttt{d3} comparison used here, for instance by evaluating additional model sizes or alternative probabilistic formulations, would help establish whether the scaling advantage observed for flow matching continues to hold at larger capacities or eventually saturates in the same way the deterministic models appear to. Fourth, improving the complexity of information inflow to current model architecture could yield higher accuracy. For instance, including pressure or density information of the system or using a discretized grid as input enables more complex modelling and thus the possiblity of better quality predictions. Finallly, it would be interesting to observe model quality on fully unseen data, or use the model in a transfer learning approach to fine-tune on new similar datasets, as is often done with \textit{PDE foundation models}.
