\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

This work aimed to build a collection of foundation models for Navier-Stokes dynamics that is both rollout-stable and physically consistent across a wide domain space of flow regimes, relying solely on velocity-field inputs. Secondly it investigated whether a probabilistic, flow-matching-based formulation performs better than a deterministic autoregressive baseline in achieving this goal. The results presented in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} allow each of the three research questions posed in the introduction to be answered directly.

\section{Answering the Research Questions}

\paragraph{RQ1: Single-step prediction accuracy.} The first research question asked whether probabilistic flow matching models can outperform deterministic baselines in predicting subsequent system states when trained on a heterogeneous, multi-source dataset spanning a wide variety of physical parameters and flow regimes. The single-step results in Section~\ref{fig:ssbox} confirm that this is the case for the majority of the evaluated datasets: the flow matching model \texttt{FM-d2} achieves a lower relative absolute error than its deterministic counterpart on nearly all benchmarks, consistent with the hypothesis that a distributional formulation is better suited to capturing the sensitive and at times multi-modal nature of the underlying solution space. This advantage was not universal, however. On the \texttt{pdegym-sines} dataset the deterministic model matched or exceeded the flow matching model, a discrepancy attributed to a mismatch between the dominant frequencies present in the data and the frequency initialisation of the flow matching prior. This exception, together with the observation that additional model capacity is absorbed more productively by the probabilistic architecture than by the deterministic one, indicates that the benefit of probabilistic modelling is real but conditional on reasonable alignment between the learned prior and the target data distribution, rather than an unconditional property of the flow matching formulation itself.

\paragraph{RQ2: Long-horizon rollout stability.} The second research question concerned how autoregressive rollout error accumulates over time for the two model families, and whether the probabilistic formulation confers greater long-horizon stability across datasets of varying temporal length. The rollout results in Section~\ref{fig:rollout} show that the flow matching models accumulate error more slowly than the deterministic models on the majority of datasets, producing rollouts that remain closer to a physically plausible regime for longer. This effect is compounded by an asymmetry in how the two families scale with capacity: the deterministic models plateau across depth variants, while the deeper flow matching variant shows a marked further reduction in error accumulation relative to its smaller counterpart. Two caveats qualify this picture. First, the \texttt{pdegym-sines} dataset again favours the deterministic model, mirroring the single-step exception and reinforcing the frequency-mismatch explanation. Second, external, unobserved forcing in the \texttt{pdebench-incomp} dataset degrades rollout accuracy for all models regardless of formulation, showing that probabilistic modelling improves but does not eliminate the difficulty of predicting dynamics driven by unobserved inputs. Taken together, the evidence supports an affirmative but conditional answer: the probabilistic formulation does confer greater long-horizon stability, most clearly on datasets where the flow matching prior is well matched to the data, and less clearly where that alignment breaks down or where unmodelled physical forcing dominates the error budget.

\paragraph{RQ3: Physical and spectral consistency.} The third research question asked whether the predicted system states satisfy physical characteristics as measured by spectral analysis, and whether probabilistic models produce spectrally more consistent predictions than deterministic ones. The spectral analysis in Section~\ref{se:spectraresults} directly supports this claim. Across the large-scale, inertial, and dissipation ranges, both models capture the underlying physics with only small deviations from the reference spectra, but the flow matching model's median and IQR sit consistently and significantly below the deterministic baseline's error, both on short spatial scales and over long temporal horizons. This is not limited to translational kinetic energy: the enstrophy spectrum computed on the \textit{amira} dataset shows the same pattern, indicating that the probabilistic model also reproduces the rotational, vortical structure of the flow more faithfully than the deterministic model. As with the previous two research questions, the \texttt{pdegym-sines} dataset is the recurring exception, where the deterministic model attains comparable or locally lower spectral error, consistent with the frequency-mismatch hypothesis raised throughout the chapter. Overall, point-wise accuracy gains for the flow matching model are corroborated, rather than contradicted, by the spectral evidence: the probabilistic samples are not merely closer to the reference trajectory pointwise, but reproduce the correct distribution of energy across scales, which is a stronger and more physically meaningful notion of fidelity.

\section{Summary}

Across all three research questions, a consistent narrative emerges. Probabilistic flow matching provides a measurable advantage over deterministic autoregressive prediction in single-step accuracy, rollout stability, and spectral fidelity, and this advantage grows with model capacity in a way that the deterministic formulation does not replicate. The mechanism behind this advantage appears to be the same in each case: learning a distribution over plausible future states, rather than a single point estimate, better matches the sensitive and multi-modal character of turbulent and compressible Navier-Stokes dynamics, and this benefit compounds over autoregressive rollout steps and is visible not only in bulk error statistics but in the detailed distribution of energy across spatial scales. At the same time, the recurrence of the \texttt{pdegym-sines} exception across all three research questions is itself an informative result: it shows that the advantage of probabilistic modelling is not unconditional, but depends on a reasonable correspondence between the frequency content of the data and the initialisation of the flow matching prior. When this correspondence breaks down, the deterministic model's simpler, more direct mapping can become competitive or superior. This suggests that the benefit of the probabilistic approach used in this work is a property of how well the prior distribution matches the target dynamics, rather than an intrinsic and universal property of probabilistic modelling per se.

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Prior specification.} The flow matching model's sensitivity to the frequency content of the target data, most visible in the \texttt{pdegym-sines} exception, points to a limitation in how the prior distribution is currently specified. The prior is not adapted per dataset, and its mismatch with high-frequency, sharply-varying fields such as sine-wave initial conditions appears to directly limit performance in exactly the regime where sharp interfaces need to be preserved.

\paragraph{Unobserved forcing.} None of the models evaluated in this work, deterministic or probabilistic, are provided with information about external body forcing where it is present, as demonstrated by the elevated rollout error on the \texttt{pdebench-incomp} dataset. This is a limitation of the input representation used across the whole study rather than of either model family specifically, and it caps the achievable rollout accuracy on any dataset where such unobserved drivers are active.

\paragraph{Scope of evaluation.} The spectral analysis in this work focuses primarily on the \textit{amira}, \textit{pdegym-sines}, and \textit{pdegym-bb} datasets for the energy spectrum, with the enstrophy spectrum evaluated in the main chapter only for \textit{amira}, and full results for the remaining datasets deferred to the appendix. While the reported trends are consistent with those found in the appendix, the depth of analysis for the wider dataset collection is comparatively limited in the main text.

\section{Future Work}

Building on the limitations above, three directions appear most promising. First, adapting or learning the flow matching prior per dataset, or conditioning its frequency content on statistics of the input data, could close the gap observed on the \texttt{pdegym-sines} dataset and test directly whether the exception is indeed attributable to prior misspecification rather than some other property of that benchmark. Second, incorporating known or inferred forcing terms as an additional model input would allow a cleaner assessment of how much of the remaining rollout error on forced datasets such as \texttt{pdebench-incomp} is attributable to the unobserved-driver problem versus genuine limitations of the surrogate dynamics model. Third, extending the depth-scaling analysis beyond the \texttt{d2}/\texttt{d3} comparison used here, for instance by evaluating additional model sizes or alternative probabilistic formulations, would help establish whether the scaling advantage observed for flow matching continues to hold at larger capacities or eventually saturates in the same way the deterministic models appear to.

\section{Closing Remarks}

This work provides evidence that probabilistic, flow-matching-based surrogate models can outperform deterministic autoregressive baselines on Navier--Stokes prediction tasks not only in aggregate error, but in the more demanding sense of reproducing the correct distribution of energy and enstrophy across spatial scales, and that this advantage persists and strengthens over long autoregressive rollouts. These findings support the use of probabilistic formulations as a promising direction for physically consistent foundation models of fluid dynamics, while also clarifying, through the recurring \texttt{pdegym-sines} exception, that this advantage is conditional on the learned prior being reasonably well matched to the dynamics of the target system.
