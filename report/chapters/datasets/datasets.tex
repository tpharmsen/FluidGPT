\section{Dataset Selection}
\label{se:datasetselection}
 
Based on the criteria outlined in Section~\ref{se:datasetconditions}, nine datasets were selected for this study. Their key characteristics are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:datasetproperties}. Collectively the raw data amount to approximately 21\,TB.
 
The selected datasets span a broad region of the Navier-Stokes problem space, covering a diverse range of flow regimes, boundary conditions, external forcing mechanisms, and initialisation procedures. This diversity is a deliberate design choice: training a foundation model that generalises across fluid-dynamics scenarios requires exposure to qualitatively different dynamics during training, rather than a large but narrow corpus drawn from a single regime.
 
For each dataset, 80\,\% of the available trajectories were assigned to the training split; the remaining 20\,\% were held out for validation and zero-shot evaluation. Prior to training, all datasets were preprocessed to enforce a common spatial and temporal resolution and to enable efficient data loading, as described in Section~\ref{se:preprocessing}. For both training and validation, only the velocity fields ($V_x$, $V_y$) were retained; all other channels (e.g., pressure, density, tracers, etc.) were discarded to minimise storage and computational overhead and to challenge the model to infer missing information from the velocity fields alone.

\subsection{AMIRASET}
\label{ss:amiraset}
 
AMIRASET is a large-scale ensemble of 8000 unsteady two-dimensional flows generated with the Gerris flow solver~\citep{Jakob2021FluidFlowDataset}. All simulations are performed on a regular $512^2$ grid with spatially periodic boundary conditions. The ensemble spans a wide dynamic range: Reynolds numbers vary from $1$ to $4096$ and kinematic viscosities from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$, ordering trajectories from laminar to fully turbulent regimes. The raw data, stored in the \texttt{.am} format at a resolution of $512 \times 512 \times 1001$ time steps, occupy approximately 16\,TB; they are subsequently downsampled and converted to \texttt{.hdf5} as part of the preprocessing pipeline described in Section~\ref{se:preprocessing}.

\subsection{PDEBENCH}
\label{ss:pdebench}
 
Two datasets from the PDEBench benchmark suite~\citep{takamoto2022pdebench} are included.
 
\paragraph{PDEBench-2DCNS.}
This dataset contains 44000 trajectories of two-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes simulations in the turbulence-dominant regime. Dynamic and bulk viscosities each vary independently over the range $[10^{-8},\,10^{-1}]$, and the flow speed spans Mach numbers from $0.1$ to $1.0$, capturing both subsonic and transonic conditions. Simulations employ periodic boundary conditions on grids ranging from $128^2$ to $512^2$. This model is particularly valuable for exposing the foundation model to compressibility effects, which are absent in the other datasets. As only the velocity components of the flow are retained for training, the model must learn to infer density and pressure effects indirectly from the velocity fields. The raw data, stored in \texttt{.hdf5} format at resolutions of $128 \times 128 \times 21$ to $512 \times 512 \times 21$ time steps, occupy approximately 4.4\,TB. they are subsequently downsampled and converted to a uniform resolution of $128^2$ as part of the preprocessing pipeline described in Section~\ref{se:preprocessing}. \paragraph{PDEBench-2DINS.}
This dataset contains 1100 trajectories of two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes simulations with external forcing. \subsection{PDEGYM}
\label{ss:pdegym}
 
Six datasets from the PDEGym collection are included. All simulations were generated with the AZEBAN spectral hyperviscosity solver, a pseudo-spectral code for incompressible flows that enforces periodic boundary conditions on a uniform space-time grid. Each dataset contains approximately 20000 trajectories of two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes flow at a spatial resolution of $128^2$ with 21 temporal snapshots per trajectory. The kinematic viscosity is fixed at $\nu = 4 \times 10^{-4}$ across all six datasets.
 
The datasets differ solely in their initialisation scheme, providing a controlled study of how initial conditions affect downstream flow statistics. The six schemes are: Gaussian random fields, sine waves, piecewise-constant vorticity fields, Brownian bridges, shear layers, and sine vortex sheets. The first two schemes are commonly used in the literature to generate initial conditions for turbulence simulations, while the remaining four are novel schemes designed to produce qualitatively different flow structures and statistics. The raw data, stored in \texttt{.nc} format at a resolution of $128 \times 128 \times 21$ time steps are subsequently converted to \texttt{.hdf5} as part of the preprocessing pipeline described in Section~\ref{se:preprocessing}.

\section{Preprocessing}
\label{se:preprocessing}
 
All datasets are preprocessed offline before training begins to produce a unified body of data at a common spatial resolution and temporal cadence. The preprocessing pipeline consists of three stages: spatial interpolation, temporal subsampling, and file-format conversion. Dedicated preprocessing scripts were written for each source dataset to execute these stages in a single pass, avoiding redundant computation during training. The preprocessed data is stored on disk in a format that supports efficient random-access loading during training, as described in Section~\ref{ss:fileformat}. As the preprocessed datasets are still too large to fit in GPU memory, the data loader fetches batches of trajectories from disk on-the-fly during training.

\subsection{Spatial Interpolation}
\label{ss:spatialinterp}
 
Source simulations are defined on the unit square $[0,1]^2$ at spatial resolutions ranging from $128^2$ to $512^2$ (see Table~\ref{tab:datasetproperties}). Because the model architecture expects a fixed-size spatial input, all fields are interpolated to a common grid of $128^2$ grid points the minimum resolution present in the union of datasets. Within the dataset, the datapoints are placed in a homogenous manner, but do not line up with the downsampled grid points in the unit square, so custom fourier interpolation is applied to map each source field onto the target uniform grid.
 
% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Temporal Subsampling}
\label{ss:temporalsamp}

The model assumes a fixed temporal stride of $\delta T = 0.05$ between consecutive states,
corresponding to 20 snapshots per unit time length (excluding the endpoint), or 21 snapshots
when the endpoint is included. This rate of 20 snapshots per unit interval represents the
minimum sampling frequency across all datasets, and is therefore adopted as the common target
cadence. For datasets whose simulations span exactly $[0, 1]$, this yields trajectories of
21 snapshots. For datasets with longer simulation time ranges, the total trajectory length is
proportionally larger, but the temporal density after subsampling remains the same 20
snapshots per unit time length throughout.

Fortunately, the native sampling frequencies of all source datasets share a common factor
with the target cadence of 20 snapshots per unit time, meaning temporal alignment is achieved
by uniform subsampling rather than interpolation. This avoids the introduction of any
interpolation artefacts in the preprocessed trajectories.

\subsection{File-Format Conversion and Storage}
\label{ss:fileformat}
 
The source datasets are distributed in several formats: \texttt{.am}, \texttt{.h5}, \texttt{.hdf5}, and \texttt{.nc}. Their combined size precludes caching preprocessed data in GPU memory. To support efficient random-access loading during training, all preprocessed trajectories are stored individually as \texttt{.hdf5} files, one file per trajectory. This layout allows the data loader to fetch arbitrary trajectories without reading or decompressing unrelated data.
 
During conversion, only the horizontal and vertical velocity components ($V_x$, $V_y$) are retained; all other channels are discarded to minimise storage footprint and to challenge the model to infer missing information from the velocity fields alone. For datasets distributed in the proprietary \texttt{.am} format (i.e., AMIRASET), no existing parsing library was available, so a custom binary parser was implemented.
 
Alongside the per-trajectory files, a metadata file is written for each dataset recording per-channel statistics (mean and standard deviation). These statistics are used both for input normalisation during training and for efficient data prefetching.
