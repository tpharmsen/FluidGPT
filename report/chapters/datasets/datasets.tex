\section{Dataset Selection}
\label{se:datasetselection}
 
Based on the selection criteria outlined in Section~\ref{se:datasetconditions}, nine distinct datasets were chosen for this study. Their key physical and computational characteristics are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:datasetproperties}. Collectively, the raw data adds up to approximately $21~\text{TB}$ of simulated fluid trajectories. The selected datasets span a broad region of the Navier-Stokes problem space, covering a diverse range of flow regimes, boundary conditions, external forcing mechanisms, and initialisation procedures. This structural diversity is a deliberate design choice: training a foundation model capable of generalising across fluid-dynamics scenarios requires exposure to qualitatively different transport phenomena during training, rather than a large but narrow-domain dataset drawn from a single regime. The challenge here is to prepare these heterogeneous datasets in a way that preserves their unique characteristics while ensuring compatibility with the model architecture and training pipeline, which is described in this section.
 
\input{chapters/datasets/datasets_specs}

For each dataset, $80\%$ of the available trajectories were assigned to the training split, while the remaining $20\%$ were held out for validation and zero-shot evaluation. Prior to training, all datasets were preprocessed to enforce a common spatial and temporal resolution and to enable efficient, parallelised data loading, as described in Section~\ref{se:preprocessing}. For both training and validation, only the velocity fields ($V_x$, $V_y$) were retained, all other physical channels (e.g., pressure, density, passive tracers) were discarded. This minimizes storage and computational overhead while challenging the foundation model to implicitly infer latent fields and missing structural information from the evolution of the velocity field alone.

\subsection{Amira}
\label{ss:amiraset}
 
This dataset comprises a large-scale ensemble of $8000$ unsteady, two-dimensional fluid flows generated using the open-source Gerris flow solver~\citep{Jakob2021FluidFlowDataset}. All simulations are performed on a regular $512^2$ grid under spatially periodic boundary conditions. The ensemble spans a wide dynamic range: Reynolds numbers vary from $1$ to $4096$ and kinematic viscosities range from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-4}$, transitioning individual trajectories from smooth laminar flows to fully developed, decaying two-dimensional turbulence. 

The simulations are initialised using a random distribution of localized vortices, which seed complex vortex interactions. The raw data, originally stored in the native \texttt{.am} format at a resolution of $512 \times 512 \times 1001$ time steps, occupy approximately $16~\text{TB}$. As part of the preprocessing pipeline described in Section~\ref{se:preprocessing}, these trajectories are sub-sampled temporally and converted to the \texttt{.hdf5} format. Due to the lack of an official name for this dataset in the literature, it is now referred to as the \textit{amira} dataset, referencing its source file extension. A sample trajectory demonstrating the decaying vortex dynamics is visualised in Figure~\ref{fig:amira}.

\subsection{PDEBench}
\label{ss:pdebench}
 
Two datasets from the PDEBench benchmark suite~\citep{takamoto2022pdebench} are included into the training data to expose the model to complex compressible behaviour and include incompressible bounded-box behavior.
 
\paragraph{PDEBench-2DCNS}
This dataset consists of $44000$ trajectories of two-dimensional compressible fluid simulations operating in a turbulence-dominant regime, governed by the compressible Navier-Stokes equations. The dynamic and bulk viscosities are varied independently over the range $[10^{-8},\,10^{-1}]$, and the flow speeds span Mach numbers from $0.1$ to $1.0$, effectively capturing both subsonic and transonic flow conditions. Simulations are executed with periodic boundary conditions on grids ranging from $128^2$ to $512^2$. This dataset is uniquely valuable for exposing the foundation model to compressibility shocks and dilatational velocity fields, which are absent in the other fluid subsets. Because only the velocity fields are exposed to the model, it must learn to reconstruct density fluctuations and pressure waves indirectly from velocity divergence and gradients.

The dataset features two distinct initialization regimes designed to seed varied spectral profiles:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Random Field Initialization:} The initial velocity field $u_0(\mathbf{x})$ is constructed as a superposition of sinusoidal modes (Figure~\ref{fig:compa}),
    \begin{equation}
        u_0(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{i} A_i \sin(k_i \mathbf{x} + \phi_i),
        \label{eq:random_ic}
    \end{equation}
    where $k_i = 2\pi n_i / L$ represents the wave numbers with $n_i$ sampled uniformly from $[1, n_{\mathrm{max}}]$, $A_i \in [0,1]$ are random amplitudes, and $\phi_i \in (0, 2\pi)$ are random phases. The initial mass density and pressure fields are constructed by superimposing a uniform background value onto this perturbation field.
    
    \item \textbf{Turbulent Field Initialization:} The initial velocity field is initialized with a structured power-law spectrum (Figure~\ref{fig:compb}),
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}, t=0) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} A_i \sin(k_i \mathbf{x} + \phi_i),
        \label{eq:turb_ic}
    \end{equation}
    utilising $n = 4$ primary modes with amplitude scaling defined by $A_i = \bar{v}/|k|^d$, where $d=2$ for the two-dimensional domain, and the characteristic velocity scale is bounded by the local speed of sound ($\bar{v} = c_s M$, with $c_s = \sqrt{\Gamma p / \rho}$). To suppress unphysical, spurious acoustic shocks at $t=0$, the compressible component of the velocity field is mathematically removed via a Helmholtz decomposition in Fourier space, retaining strictly the divergence-free component for the initial state.
\end{enumerate}

The raw data is stored in \texttt{.hdf5} format across spatial dimensions from $128 \times 128 \times 21$ to $512 \times 512 \times 21$ spatio-temporal steps. All trajectories are downsampled to a uniform spatial resolution of $128^2$ during preprocessing, which is explained in Section~\ref{se:preprocessing}. This dataset is hereafter referred to as \textit{pdebench-comp}. Sample temporal evolutions from both initialisation conditions are presented in Figure~\ref{fig:comp} for easier conceptualization.

\paragraph{PDEBench-2DINS (Incompressible Navier-Stokes with Forcing).}
This dataset contains $1100$ trajectories tracking two-dimensional incompressible flows progressed with a continuous, deterministic external forcing function. The physical domain enforces static Dirichlet boundary conditions on a regular $512^2$ grid, including different boundary interactions to the full dataset compared to the other datasets. The initial state is generated from a smooth, low-frequency random field, which subsequently undergoes non-linear deformation driven by a fixed Taylor-Green-like or stochastic forcing term. Although the explicit algebraic structure of the external force is not included in the model inputs, exposing the neural architecture to these forced trajectories forces it to interpolate forcing behaviour if multiple subsequent system states are used as input. Also, unique boundary conditions ensure generalisation across different boundary conditions. The raw data ($1.1~\text{TB}$ in \texttt{.h5} format at $512 \times 512 \times 1000$ steps) are downsampled to the uniform training resolution and format as detailed in Section~\ref{se:preprocessing}. This subset is referred to as \textit{pdebench-incomp}, with a sample sequence depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:pdebench-incomp}.

\subsection{PDEGym}
\label{ss:pdegym}
 
To systematically evaluate the foundation model's capability to generalize across distinct structural topologies, six datasets from the PDEGym collection, originally created for the Poseidon foundation model benchmark, are incorporated. All simulations were generated via the AZEBAN spectral hyperviscosity solver, a high-order pseudo-spectral framework for incompressible fluid variants that enforces exact periodic boundary conditions on a uniform space-time grid. Each of the six datasets contains approximately $20000$ trajectories of two-dimensional incompressible Navier-Stokes flow resolved at a spatial resolution of $128^2$ with $21$ temporal snapshots. The kinematic viscosity is held constant at $\nu = 4 \times 10^{-4}$ across all configurations. These datasets do not focus on variability of physical parameters, but rather on the diversity of initial conditions and the resulting flow structures. The datasets are designed to challenge the model's ability to capture a wide range of hydrodynamic phenomena, including vortex interactions, shear instabilities, and turbulent cascades.

The six datasets differ exclusively in their initialisation schemes, which is described below.
\begin{enumerate}\label{no:schemes}
    \item \textbf{Gaussian Random Fields:} Initialized via a standard homogeneous and isotropic Gaussian random field with a predefined power-law energy spectrum, producing variation across vortices that mimic classic isotropic decaying turbulence. This datasaet will be referred to as \textit{pdegym-gauss}.
    \item \textbf{Sine Waves:} Initialized using a clean combination of low-order deterministic sinusoidal wave modes, resulting in highly structured regular vortex arrays that gradually destabilise. This dataset will be referred to as \textit{pdegym-sines}.
    \item \textbf{Shear Layers:} Initialized with parallel fluid streams moving at different velocities, forming a localized velocity gradient that quickly rolls up into discrete vortices via the Kelvin-Helmholtz instability mechanism. This dataset will be referred to as \textit{pdegym-sl}.
    \item \textbf{Sine Vortex Sheets:} Initialized with an alternating array of concentrated vortex sheets, seeding high-frequency shear dynamics and local dissipation patterns. This dataset will be referred to as \textit{pdegym-svs}.
    \item \textbf{Brownian Bridges:} Initialized using a constrained stochastic process that enforces rough, non-differentiable initial profiles, providing a benchmark for the model's robustness against high-frequency spatial noise and non-smooth fields. This dataset will be referred to as \textit{pdegym-bb}.
    \item \textbf{Piecewise-Constant Vorticity:} Initialized with discrete patches of uniform vorticity bounded by sharp discontinuities. This configuration exercises the model's ability to propagate sharp boundaries and capture coherent vortex mergers without numerical diffusion. This dataset will be referred to as \textit{pdegym-pwc}.
\end{enumerate}

The raw data, originally stored in the \texttt{.nc} (NetCDF) format, are converted directly to the unified \texttt{.hdf5} standard pipeline without spatial downsampling. Representative initialisations and their subsequent states are detailed in Figure~\ref{fig:all}.

\section{Preprocessing}
\label{se:preprocessing}
 
All datasets are preprocessed offline before training begins to produce a unified body of data at a common spatial resolution and temporal cadence. The preprocessing pipeline consists of three stages: spatial interpolation, temporal subsampling, and file-format conversion. Dedicated preprocessing scripts were written for each source dataset to execute these stages in a single pass, avoiding redundant computation during training. The preprocessed data is stored on disk in a format that supports efficient random-access loading during training, as described in Section~\ref{ss:fileformat}. As the preprocessed datasets are still too large to fit in GPU memory, the data loader fetches batches of trajectories from disk on-the-fly during training.

As for the training data, no data augmentation is applied during preprocessing. The focus of this work is on evaluating the model's ability to learn from the raw, unprocessed trajectories and to generalise across different flow regimes and initial conditions, rather than on artificially increasing the dataset size through transformations, which is commonly done in training foundation models. As such, the preprocessed datasets are intended to be as close as possible to the original raw data, with only the necessary adjustments for spatial and temporal alignment and efficient storage.

The normalisation of the velocity fields is performed on-the-fly during training using the mean and standard deviation computed from the training split of each dataset, which are stored in the metadata files generated during preprocessing. This allows the model to learn from the raw velocity values while ensuring that the input data is appropriately scaled for training stability and convergence.

\subsection{Spatial Interpolation}
\label{ss:spatialinterp}
 
Source simulations are defined on the unit square $[0,1]^2$ at spatial resolutions ranging from $128^2$ to $512^2$ (see Table~\ref{tab:datasetproperties}). Because the model architecture expects a fixed-size spatial input, all fields are interpolated to a common grid of $128^2$ grid points the minimum resolution present in the union of datasets. Within the dataset, the datapoints are placed in a homogenous manner, but do not line up with the downsampled grid points in the unit square, so custom fourier interpolation is applied to map each source field onto the target uniform grid.
 
% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Temporal Subsampling}
\label{ss:temporalsamp}

The model assumes a fixed temporal stride of $\delta T = 0.05$ between consecutive states,
corresponding to 20 snapshots per unit time length (excluding the endpoint), or 21 snapshots
when the endpoint is included. This rate of 20 snapshots per unit interval represents the
minimum sampling frequency across all datasets, and is therefore adopted as the common target
cadence. For datasets whose simulations span exactly $[0, 1]$, this yields trajectories of
21 snapshots. For datasets with longer simulation time ranges, the total trajectory length is
proportionally larger, but the temporal density after subsampling remains the same 20
snapshots per unit time length throughout.

Fortunately, the native sampling frequencies of all source datasets share a common factor
with the target cadence of 20 snapshots per unit time, meaning temporal alignment is achieved
by uniform subsampling rather than interpolation. This avoids the introduction of any
interpolation artefacts in the preprocessed trajectories.

\subsection{File-Format Conversion and Storage}
\label{ss:fileformat}

The source datasets are distributed in several formats: \texttt{.am}, \texttt{.h5}, \texttt{.hdf5}, and \texttt{.nc}. Their combined size precludes caching preprocessed data in GPU memory. To support efficient random-access loading during training, all preprocessed trajectories are stored individually as \texttt{.hdf5} files, one file per trajectory. This layout allows the data loader to fetch arbitrary trajectories without reading or decompressing unrelated data. Alongside the per-trajectory files, a metadata file is written for each dataset recording dataset-specific statistics used for normalisation and quick-access indexing. 

During conversion, only the horizontal and vertical velocity components ($V_x$, $V_y$) are retained; all other channels are discarded to minimise storage footprint and to challenge the model to infer missing information from the velocity fields alone. For datasets distributed in the proprietary \texttt{.am} format, no existing parsing library was available, so a custom-made binary parser was implemented.
